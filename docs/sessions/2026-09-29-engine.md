# Session · 2026-09-29 · One engine, step 1 (prototype)

Decision 0010, plan step 1: one three.js view from space to a town, with no hand-over to MapLibre.
Open `web/engine.html` (dev: `http://localhost:5317/engine.html`, or `?spot=pune`, `?lng=&lat=&alt=`, `?order=level` for the old loading order).

## Built
- **Tile engine** (`web/src/engine/tiles/`). We wrote our own: `geo-three` is Web Mercator only, keeps one texture per node in metre units and has no hook for our shader. It is an EPSG:4326 quadtree (level 0 = two 180° tiles, 256 px), with screen-space-error LOD, horizon and frustum culling, an LRU cache with a budget, and sphere patches with skirts drawn relative to their centre so precision holds at 3 km. The nearest loaded ancestor is always drawn, so there are no holes. One shader draws the same Living Earth recipe as the dot globe (class colour, canopy, hillshade, monthly NDVI from the tile or from the pack). The dot hologram fades out and the surface fades in between 12,000 and 2,500 km.
- **Focus-first loading** (owner request). One priority queue, re-sorted every frame. Each tile is scored by its distance from the focus (the screen centre, or the ground under the cursor while zooming; wheel zoom now zooms toward the cursor), its level (a parent always goes before its child) and how blurred it is on screen. Requests for tiles that left the view are aborted with AbortController after 300 ms. The engine prefetches one level finer around the focus, plus what the camera will want at the flight target and a little ahead on the path. A sharper tile crossfades over its parent in 200 ms. The HUD shows queue length, in-flight, prefetch and aborted counts.
- **Camera** (`web/src/engine/camera/`): 25,000 km to 3 km. Zoom is a constant ratio per notch and pan is scaled by altitude. Inertia damps harder close in. Near and far planes hug the visible ground (far/near stays under 3,000 at town scale). Flights run in log altitude with a rise for long hops.
- **Labels** (`web/src/engine/labels/`): HTML labels in Fraunces from our own place-name product. They are ranked by population, with capitals and admin seats boosted. The smallest population shown depends on altitude. Screen-space greedy collision runs on a grid, labels fade in and out, and it tops out at 70 labels.
- **Data products** (pipeline): world tiles for levels 0–4 cut from pack v2 (`uv run atlas tiles-world`, 513 files, 27 MB), and detail levels 5–8 from Earth Engine (`.github/workflows/tiles.yml`). The detail channels are WorldCover class, MODIS tree cover, Copernicus GLO-30 hillshade and 12 monthly NDVI frames. The job is a matrix of level-3 shards that skips open ocean and resumes with `gh run rerun --failed`. Place names: GeoNames cities1000 worldwide, 34k major places up front and 137k in 89 chunks (`uv run atlas places`). Every product has a manifest with sources.

## Known issues
- **Town scale is soft.** At 10 km, one level-8 pixel (~300 m) covers ~40 screen pixels. WorldCover is 10 m, so levels 10–12 (~75–20 m) are possible, but each level is 4x the tiles.
- **Exact WorldCover mode is slow in Earth Engine**: ~25 s per level-8 tile call, which is ~9 hours of Earth Engine time for the world at the 40-call concurrency limit. The mode pyramid (used at levels 5–6) is about 100x faster but less exact.
- **Tile-edge seams.** NDVI and hillshade are clamped at tile edges, so faint seams can show. Hillshade also has seams every 22.5° at shard edges.
- Water and snow are still the globe pack's ~35 km layers and fade out below 2,500 km. Up close, water comes only from the WorldCover class. Monthly water and snow tile channels are next.
- Frame times here come from CPU rasterisers (SwiftShader and llvmpipe), because WSL headless Chrome has no GPU. They show relative cost, not phone fps.
